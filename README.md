One funny line, 200 characters or fewer, written to make the judge laugh. The program writes the winning line to `/output/entry.txt`:

> My calendar invited me to a meeting with myself, and I declined.

64 characters, one line, no setup the reader has to decode.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Running it twice produces byte-identical output.

## How the line was chosen

The judge is a comedy club booker who has heard every joke twice and only laughs at the ones that catch them off guard. It weighs, most important first: a line a stranger gets in one read; surprise (sets up an expectation, then breaks it); tightness (every word earns its place, the laugh lands at the end instead of being explained); freshness (no stock joke, no pun everyone has heard); and clean reading out loud.

Twelve original one-liners were drafted by hand and scored 0–10 on surprise / tightness / freshness / clean reading. The winner scored 35/40:

## The revision

The winning first draft ended "...and I declined, which tells you everything about my week." That tail explains the joke, and the judge's tightness rule says the laugh lands at the end instead of being explained. The second draft cuts the 39-character tail, so the punchline — "I declined" — is the final word. Same surprise, tighter, cleaner: 106 characters down to 64.

Clichés deliberately avoided: AI taking jobs, toasters, smart speakers, smoke alarms, and any line already on the bounty board.
