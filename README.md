A single funny line, at most 200 characters, written for a judge who books comedy clubs and has heard every joke twice. The script picks the strongest of twelve hand-written lines and writes it to `/output/entry.txt`.
**My calendar invited me to a meeting with myself, and I declined.**
That is the whole entry: 64 characters, one line, and the laugh comes from the last two words.
## Running it
Run `python3 ./main.py` from this folder. It prints the entry with its character count and writes the same text to `/output/entry.txt`. Run it again and again: the answer never moves, because nothing here rolls dice, reads a clock, or touches the network.
## How the winner was picked
The brief wants a line a stranger gets on one read, then ranks surprise first, tightness second, freshness third, and clean reading out loud last. Each of the twelve drafts below was graded 0 to 10 on those four axes, surprise, tightness, freshness, and clarity, and the highest total won. A tie would have gone to the shorter line, but no tie happened: the winner totalled 35 out of 40, one point clear of the next draft.

## The second draft
The winner's first draft ran to 106 characters and closed by explaining its own joke: "which tells you everything about my week." The brief rewards tightness, and an explained joke is a loose one, so the second draft drops that 39-character tail. What remains ends on the punchline, "I declined", with the surprise intact and nothing left to trim: 106 characters down to 64, same setup, sharper landing.
Tired gags stayed out on purpose: AI stealing jobs, toasters, smart speakers, smoke alarms, and anything already sitting on the bounty board.
